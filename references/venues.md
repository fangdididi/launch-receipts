# Venue cards

These cards describe how each venue usually works. **Rules change. Open the venue's own rules page, sidebar or pinned post on the day you plan, and write what you read into `VENUES.md` with the date.** If the card and the live rules disagree, the live rules win.

Each card: who is there, what counts as on-topic, hard rules, format, what it rewards, common ways to get removed.

---

## Show HN (Hacker News)

- **Who**: developers, founders, technical generalists. Curious, quick to test claims.
- **On-topic**: something you made that people can try now: software they can run, a site they can use, hardware with a video, a book with a sample chapter. Not on-topic: landing pages, signup pages, newsletters, blog posts, lists, fundraisers. Minor version bumps are usually not enough; a major overhaul can be.
- **Hard rules**: title starts with `Show HN: `. Do not ask anyone to upvote or comment. You must be the maker and be around to discuss it. Make trying easy, ideally without signup.
- **Format**: title (80 characters max), URL to the thing itself, optional text. A short first comment or text explaining why you built it and what is unusual about the approach works well.
- **Rewards**: an honest story, technical detail, something unusual, a maker who answers well.
- **Removed or ignored when**: it is a waitlist, it reads like marketing, the maker argues with critics.

## Product Hunt

- **Who**: early adopters, makers, product people, some investors.
- **On-topic**: a product people can use or sign up for, including early-stage ones (mark as not yet available if so).
- **Hard rules**: never ask for upvotes, never offer anything in return for votes, no coordinated voting or mass DMs. Sharing the link and asking people to try and comment is fine.
- **Format**: name, tagline (60 characters), description (260 characters), gallery images (two or more), optional video, pricing tag, a maker's first comment. Launch days run on Pacific time; scheduled launches go live at 12:01 AM PT.
- **Rewards**: a clear first comment (who it is for, why you built it, what feedback you want), a maker present all day, replies that use the product.
- **Removed or ignored when**: vote manipulation, marketing-speak, a gallery of mockups instead of the real product.

## Reddit

- **Who**: depends entirely on the subreddit. The communities where your users spend time beat startup subreddits full of other founders.
- **On-topic**: whatever that subreddit's rules say. Common patterns: no promotion at all; a pinned weekly or monthly share thread only; one day a week with a flair; a self-promotion flair; moderator approval first.
- **Hard rules**: Reddit's spam policy prohibits repeated or unsolicited mass posting; many subreddits still cite the old 9:1 idea (most of your activity should not be about your own stuff). Account age and karma minimums are common. Some subs count a friend's project as self-promotion.
- **Format**: title plus text post; lead with what you learned or built and why, link near the end, disclose that it is yours.
- **Rewards**: being a regular there, writing for that community, staying for the comments.
- **Removed when**: wrong day, wrong thread, missing flair, the same text in five subreddits, drive-by posting.

## X (Twitter)

- **Who**: your own followers first; reach beyond them is luck plus quotes from others.
- **Format**: 280 weighted characters per post (CJK characters count double, each link counts 23). A short video or GIF of the product doing its job does more than any adjective.
- **Rewards**: a concrete moment ("it renamed 11 years of photos and left an undo file"), a reply thread with details, answering every early reply.
- **Avoid**: mass tagging, reply-guy promotion under strangers' posts, engagement-bait.

## Bluesky / Mastodon

- **Who**: developer and open-source communities, often allergic to hype.
- **Format**: Bluesky 300 characters; Mastodon instances commonly 500. Use alt text on images. Hashtags matter more on Mastodon.
- **Rewards**: plain language, open-source details, alt text.

## Lobsters

- **Who**: technical readers; invitation-only accounts.
- **Notes**: posting your own work is accepted when you are an active participant; tag it with the authored-by-you option. Do not use it if you are not already part of the community.

## Newsletters and podcasts in your niche

- **Who**: curated audiences. One mention from a trusted curator can beat a front page.
- **Format**: a short, personal pitch to the editor: one sentence what, one sentence why their readers, try link, one SHOWN receipt. No attachments, no follow-up spam. Many have a submission form; use it.

## Awesome lists and directories (including skill directories)

- **Who**: people browsing a category on purpose.
- **Hard rules**: each list has a CONTRIBUTING file; many require a minimum age, stars, docs or a license. Follow the entry format exactly, one entry per pull request.
- **Rewards**: an accurate one-line description, not a slogan.

## V2EX (分享创造 node)

- **Who**: Chinese-speaking developers and indie makers.
- **On-topic**: sharing what you made belongs in the "分享创造" node; promotional posts elsewhere are usually moved or removed.
- **Format**: plain-spoken Chinese, what it is, why you made it, how to try, what you want to hear. Screenshots welcome.
- **Rewards**: honesty about limits, quick replies, a free tier or open source.
- **Removed when**: repeated posting, ad tone, posting in unrelated nodes.

## 即刻 (Jike)

- **Who**: Chinese product people, indie makers, early adopters.
- **Format**: short casual post with a screenshot or clip; topic circles for indie products.
- **Rewards**: a personal voice, behind-the-scenes detail, follow-up updates.

## Community chats (Discord, Slack, WeChat groups)

- **Rules**: only where sharing is welcome, usually a #show-and-tell or #projects channel. Never DM members you do not know. One message, then answer replies.
